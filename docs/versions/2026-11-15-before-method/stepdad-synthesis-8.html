第6章 · 第6章 母亲的智慧：善意的陷阱

Text/16-Chapter06.xhtml#anchor-24-anchor

AI译文

过度保护的母亲与愤怒的继父聚在一起，却都不愿照镜子，会怎样？Tracy 来信说：“丈夫对我青春期女儿提出建议时，我会很防御。一部分原因是他比我严格，要求事情完全按他的方式做，我则比较放松。另一原因是他的说法。他会说：‘你最好告诉她，如果再不改正，就如何如何……’他是在命令我该告诉她什么。若他温和地说：‘我看得出你因 Rebecca 很挫败。我站在你这边，愿意帮助。我对情况有一些想法’，我就更愿意听。”Tracy 提出了好建议：温和、友善地接近彼此，而不是用拒斥的怒气。但还不止如此。

英文原文
What happens when an overprotective mother and an angry stepfather come together—and neither will look in the mirror? Tracy wrote to us at SuccessfulStepfamilies.com: “I am very defensive when my husband makes suggestions about my teenage daughters. Part of it is that he is much stricter than I; he expects jobs to be done exactly the way he would do them. I am more relaxed about this stuff. The other reason I am defensive is because of how he goes about it. He says to me, ‘You better tell her that if she doesn’t straighten up then blah, blah, blah . . .’ He TELLS me what I better tell her. If he could approach me more gently and say, ‘I can see your frustration with Rebecca. I am on your side and would like to help if I can. I have some thoughts about what’s going on,’ I’d be much more willing to listen.” Tracy makes a great suggestion: Approach one another with gentleness and kindness, not rejecting anger. But there’s more.

AI译文

明智的母亲和继父知道怎样照镜子。他们愿意检视自己的动机、情绪、信念及彼此互动，而不是指责。若 Tracy 谦卑地照镜子，也许会看到，她的内疚与自责容易引发防御；也许童年严厉的家长使她反感强硬意见。这些敏感点会迅速引燃焦虑，让她无法平静回应处境和丈夫。

英文原文
Wise moms and stepdads know how to look in the mirror. They are willing to examine their motives, emotions, and beliefs, and how they interact with each other, not point the finger of blame. If Tracy would take a humble look in the mirror, perhaps she would see that her defensiveness is easily sparked by her guilt and self-blame. Or perhaps she had a harsh parent figure in her childhood and she resents anyone who has strong opinions. These types of sensitivities can quickly ignite her anxiety and steal her ability to calmly respond to the situation and her husband.

第7章 · 第7章 收起袜子：养育基础

Text/17-Chapter07.xhtml#anchor-25-anchor

AI译文

科学项目到期前夜九点，Jason 才告诉继父 Craig，需要去商店买材料。他把项目拖到最后，然后期待继父帮忙。Craig 想了一会儿说：“很遗憾，你到现在才告诉我。你有三周做这个项目。你选择不负责任，并不意味着我应该立刻跳进去替你解决。Jason，你很聪明，我希望你学业顺利，但这次我不会救急。我相信你能想出办法。”说完，他让 Jason 自己体验拖延的后果。

英文原文
Jason waited till 9:00 p.m. the night before his science project was due to tell his stepdad, Craig, that he needed items from the store. He neglected the project till the last minute and then expected his stepdad to help out. Craig thought about it for a minute and replied, “I’m sorry that you’ve waited till now to tell me this. You’ve had three weeks to work on the project. Just because you have decided to act irresponsibly does not mean I should jump in and fix the problem for you. Jason, you’re a smart kid and I want you to do well in school. But I’m not going to bail you out this time. I’m sure you can figure something out.” And with that, he cut him loose to experience the consequences of procrastination.

AI译文

父母有时会疑惑，让孩子体验这种后果是不是残忍。不是。他们是在帮助孩子准备承担自己选择的责任，也是在邀请孩子教会自己如何管理责任。

英文原文
Parents sometimes wonder if they are being cruel to let children experience this type of consequence. They are not. They are preparing the child to accept responsibility for their choices. In addition, they are inviting the child to teach themselves how to manage their responsibilities.

第12章 · 第12章 继续经营伴侣关系

Text/22-Chapter12.xhtml#anchor-30-anchor

AI译文

“乐趣因素”的一个关键是共同休闲。换言之，分开度过的休闲时间不算。Craig 热衷高尔夫，一有机会就打球，也看电视转播，每两年买一套新球杆。妻子 Michelle 喜欢跑步和木雕。两人尊重对方的喜好，也在家中互相补位，让另一方享受爱好。然而，他们很少共同做事。各自的活动带来个人享受并有益身体健康，但缺少共同休闲会对婚姻产生负面影响。

英文原文
One key aspect of the Fun Factor is that leisure time be spent together. In other words, leisure time spent apart doesn’t count. Craig is an avid golfer. He plays a round of golf every chance possible. He also watches golf on TV and purchases a new set of clubs every two years. His wife, Michelle, enjoys running and wood carving. Each spouse respects the other’s preference and “covers the bases” at home so the other can enjoy their hobby. However, Craig and Michelle rarely do anything together. Their separate activities provide personal enjoyment and aid physical health, but a lack of shared leisure activity will negatively impact their marriage.

AI译文

关系开始时，打高尔夫的 Craig 与跑步的 Michelle 确实一起享受过晚餐、电影等快乐时光。但家庭、工作、教会、孩子运动活动及其他责任带来的压力与忙乱，挤走了这一关键因素。如果希望婚姻健康，明智的做法是恢复、找回已失去的东西。

英文原文
At the beginning of their relationship, Craig (the golfer) and Michelle (the runner) did share fun times together such as dinner and a movie. But the pressures and the frenzy of family, work, church, kids’ sporting events, and other responsibilities have squeezed out this crucial element. If they desire a healthy marriage, they would be wise to restore and recover what has been lost.

第15章 · 第15章 主动选择承担责任

Text/25-Chapter15.xhtml#anchor-33-anchor

AI译文

我一直觉得，历史上玛利亚作为救主之母获得如此多尊崇，而约瑟几乎不被提起，有些不公平。我们确实更了解她，因此可以理解。但那个原本不必当父亲的人呢？若约瑟没有选择承担，会怎样？

英文原文
I’ve long thought it a great injustice that Mary has received so much honor and attention throughout history for being the mother of the Savior while Joseph is hardly ever mentioned. We do know much more about her, so it is understandable. But what about the dad who didn’t have to be? What if Joseph didn’t?

AI译文

但他选择了。他选择去爱、照顾、接纳并非自己所生的孩子。他确实有选择——他几乎离开。但在圣灵引领下，他留下来。而那位舍弃天堂的荣耀、权能与特权，走向十字架，为希望牺牲自己的宇宙之神、救主，也被一位谦卑的继父选择了。

英文原文
But he did. He chose to love, care for, and claim the child he didn’t father. And clearly he had a choice—he almost walked away. But at the Spirit’s leading, he stayed. And the God of the Universe, who chose to give up heaven in all of its glory, power, and privilege in exchange for a cross, the Savior who chose to sacrifice himself for the sake of hope . . . was himself chosen by a humble stepdad.

AI译文

继父母常遭到负面评价。“恶毒继母”和“虐待孩子的继父”这类刻板印象始终不消失。但你可以让这些严厉判断从身上滑落，原因如下：它们总来自山脚下仰望的人。一旦你、家人和旁观者到达山顶，回头看，视角就不同了，非常不同。

英文原文
Stepparents often get a bad rap. The “wicked stepmother” and the “abusing stepfather” are stereotypes that just won’t go away. But here’s why you can let those harsh judgments roll off your back. They always come from people who are looking up from the bottom of the mountain. Once you, your family, and onlookers get to the top and look back down, the perspective is different. Very different.
